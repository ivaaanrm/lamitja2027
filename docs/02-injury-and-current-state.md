## 2. Injury assessment

**Reported symptom:** concentrated, pinpoint pain on the **lateral (exterior) aspect** of the knee.

**Working hypothesis: iliotibial band syndrome (ITBS).** Not a clinical diagnosis — see §3 for red flags and the recommendation to have it confirmed.

Focal pain on the outside of the knee is the textbook ITBS location: the lateral femoral epicondyle, roughly 2–3 cm above the joint line, where the band compresses a fat pad at ~20–30° of knee flexion — exactly the knee angle at footstrike.

### Four pieces of supporting evidence from the data

1. **The onset.** La Mitja's second half is 10+ km of continuous descent (kms 11–21 at −0.3% to −1.8% grade, steepest in the final 5 km). Sustained downhill on fatigued hip stabilisers is the canonical ITBS trigger.
2. **The declining threshold.** 13 Mar → 4.2 km. 23 Mar → 3.0 km. 4 Apr → **2.1 km**. Each return attempt shorter than the last — the ITBS signature, where pain arrives at a reproducible distance and each aggravation lowers the threshold.
3. **Cycling was tolerated.** Three rides of up to 30 km in February with zero running. Cycling keeps the knee outside the impingement window; PFPS and meniscal problems usually do not spare it that cleanly.
4. **The plateau.** Every run from May to July landed between 5.5 and 7.7 km — a ceiling was found and lived under.

### Signs it may be resolving

- 18 Aug: 8.2 km. 20 Aug: **10.1 km** — longest run since the race. The threshold has broken.
- Likely mechanism: five months of reduced running let it settle, plus substantial hiking (May 22 km/1320 m; Jul–Aug four hikes) building hip strength that was previously absent.

## 3. Red flags — would indicate something other than ITBS

Seek imaging if any of these are present, as they point toward the lateral meniscus or other structures and would materially change the training ramp:

- Locking or catching
- Knee giving way
- Visible swelling
- Pain at rest or at night
- Pain when twisting or squatting, rather than only when running

**Recommendation:** book a physio assessment. Seven months is a long course for ITBS, and confirmation is worth having before ramping volume.

## 5. Implications for the 2027 plan

See [`03-training-plan-2027.md`](03-training-plan-2027.md). The three that drive the plan's structure:

1. **Cadence rebuild to 85+ is simultaneously the injury fix and the race-form marker.** Best-evidenced ITBS intervention: raising cadence 5–10% shortens stride, cuts time in the compression zone, and lowers strain rate.
2. **Load the hip, don't stretch the band.** The ITB cannot be lengthened — it is anchored to the femur. Hip abductor and external rotator strength is the intervention; foam-rolling the band is not.
3. **The race course is this injury's worst case.** 10 km of sustained descent on tired legs. Training 22 weeks on flat ground and then meeting that descent rebuilds the exact conditions that caused the injury. Downhill tolerance must be a deliberate, progressive thread — not something avoided.
